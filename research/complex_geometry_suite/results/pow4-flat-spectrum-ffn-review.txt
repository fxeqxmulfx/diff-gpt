The current transformer differs from the new source theorem
Our Kernite complex.rs computes
    sigmoid(sqrt(|g_j|^2 + eps)) * g_j * u_j / sqrt(2),
with complex-linear gate/value/output maps. engine.rs invokes product<true>.
The new CP1 source theorem instead has the plain product g_j*u_j/sqrt(2),
inside an attention-free Rotate90 model. That source also uses a residual
coefficient of one; it is not our complete XSA/RoPE transformer.
Removing the radial gate would align the FFN operation, not automatically
transfer the entire source theorem to this transformer.

The radial factor is positive, so it preserves each product coordinate's phase.
It changes relative channel amplitudes and gradients. A common scalar would
largely cancel under output RMS, but this factor varies across channels and
RMS has nonzero epsilon. It cannot simply be discarded as an exact identity.
There is currently no measurement showing that this factor causes our miss.

What the theorem supplies
CP1 proves equality of independently defined real and complex source formulas.
CP2 preserves strict class comparisons under FINAL RMSNorm with positive
epsilon: all logits share one positive denominator. This is not a theorem
that removing other normalizers preserves training or generalization.
CP3 certifies classification if raw class margin exceeds the two-score error
budget, retaining the actual normalization denominator.
CP4 requires an ideal nuclear-norm minimum, fitting, a recovery certificate,
score-contrast alignment to the actual source model, a class margin and bounded
execution error. These are separate hypotheses. AdamW convergence or discovery
of those features is not proved. Plain product optimality is not asserted.

Why the original flat-task explanation cannot simply be transplanted
The original exact flat example is a^3+a*b, with a particular additive-character
readout. Our task is (a+b)^4. Its scalar fixed-character kernel is not flat.
A standalone Rust FP64 check gives singular values ranging from 3.48917639 to
19.48993472 and effective rank 77.21859778, instead of 97 equal singular values.

There is also a low-rank label readout for the quartic task. Let
H={1,22,75,96}, the four roots of unity modulo 97, and psi(s)=exp(2*pi*i*s/97).
Define phi(s^4)=sum_{r in H} psi(r*s). This is well-defined: every nonzero
fourth-power fiber is one H-orbit; the zero fiber has s=0. Then
    phi((a+b)^4) = sum_{r in H} psi(r*a) psi(r*b).
Each summand is rank one, and the four distinct characters are orthogonal,
giving rank four. This is an algebraic construction, not a new Lean theorem.
The native check verifies all 97 residues, all label fibers and DFT coefficients:
25 reachable labels, maximum fiber discrepancy 4.48e-16, maximum spectral
discrepancy 5.69e-14, effective rank 4.000000000000003. No such features or
intermediate targets were supplied to a trained model.

Existence of this scalar low-rank signal does not prove that the trained FFN
discovers it, that this one signal gives a sufficient linear multiclass decoder,
or that observed samples satisfy the required recovery certificate. The
note's learning-corrections section explicitly separates spectral availability,
representability, optimizer dynamics and floating-point fidelity.
