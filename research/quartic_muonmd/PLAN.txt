Quartic complex-linear optimizer diagnostic, frozen before training

Question: can the MuonMD recipe improve final held-out classification and
reduce the late fluctuations seen on (a+b)^4 mod97 with complex-linear weights?
This is a diagnostic on an already observed task, not a new generalization test.

Use exactly the complex-linear model from GPT commit910f206: width128 (64
complex coordinates), four heads, one attention+FFN block, real XSA, radial
complex-product FFN of real hidden width342, Peri RMS, RoPE, SSMax and tied E.
Keep its physical initialization and frozen arithmetic split: 4704 train,
4705 held-out, data seed1. Model/sampler seeds0,1,2; shuffle seed+10000;
batch256 with genuine short tails. Same initial parameters and batches in both arms.
Do not change the existing complex-geometry suite or run two training cohorts
concurrently. Small correctness tests may share the GPU before the queue is free.

Two arms, six runs; 100000 updates each, including after first99. Record first
crossing AND final/best/late-range metrics; convergence is not inferred from one
crossing. Observe complete train and held-out splits every19 updates. Save only
at initialization, every500 epochs, first99, budget end and explicit clean stop.
All misses and execution errors remain visible. No adaptive LR changes or retries.

Shared settings: zero warmup, global physical-gradient norm clip1, ordinary
coordinate Adam with betas(.9,.99), epsilon1e-8 wherever Adam is used, linear
decay from the peak at update0 to1e-8 at update99999. These new controls differ
from the historical constant-rate (.9,.98), warmup50, unclipped runs.

adamw-linear: peak LR0.002 for all independent coordinates; weight decay0.1.
muonmd-linear: direction momentum0.95, Nesterov, five Newton-Schulz iterations
with polynomial3.4445*x-4.775*x^3+2.0315*x^5. Gain Adam peak LR0.01; direction
peak LR0.01; tied-embedding Adam peak LR0.002; no weight decay. This is one
predeclared MuonMD setting, not a tuned optimum or an equal-budget LR sweep.

MD factorization W=diag(r) D diag(c), positive softplus row/column gains
initialized to1. Fix rho=the actual initial complex Frobenius norm of each
matrix. Normalize Muon's initial Nesterov matrix by its complex Frobenius norm
(zero maps to zero). The fixed output multiplier is rho/sqrt(min(m,n)), using
complex dimensions: nominal full-rank polar norm matching for our0.02
initialization. Do not renormalize each Muon update. Reproject the updated D
onto radius rho, then fuse with the updated gains. All factor gradients use
the old physical weights/gains and the same clipped physical gradient.

Complex adaptation: first contract gradients of [[A,-B],[B,A]] into A+iB;
use Hermitian Gram matrices, sum|z|^2 norms and real Hermitian gain derivatives.
One positive gain per complex row/column, shared across real and imaginary parts.
The four real blocks are reconstructed exactly after each physical update.
Keep the tied embedding/readout as one unconstrained Adam group. Paper2606.25971
uses untied groups and row-normalized embeddings/head; that change is not copied.
Direction/gain operations are a complex extension, not a proved Lean theorem.

Source: local papers/arXiv-2606.25971v2/main.tex, Section3, Section4.1 and
Appendices A/B/D; local Muon scalar polynomial and Nesterov convention in
optimizers/src/lean_optim/matrix.py. Lean MD factorization, storage and scale
identities do not provide optimizer convergence on this task. AdamMDW's extra
physical decay is deliberately not part of MuonMD here.

All training, tests, data, checkpoints and reports use native Rust/Kernite.
Checkpoint optimizer-specific momentum, gains, Adam moments and step/sampler
position; reject unclean windows or changed sources instead of silent replay.
